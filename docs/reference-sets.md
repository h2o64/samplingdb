# Reference sets

Every test split that is not an exact draw of a closed-form density is generated by a script in
`gen/`, then published to the Hugging Face dataset `h2o64/samplingdb`, from which `test_samples()`
downloads it (see [Data and cache](interface.md#data-and-cache)). Those scripts are maintainer
tooling: they are not installed with the package and nothing under `samplingdb/` imports them,
which keeps CmdStan and the samplers out of the install. Each one writes its set into the cache as
`<folder>/<name>_<spec_id>.npy`, the path `test_samples()` reads, next to a manifest
(`<name>_<spec_id>_manifest.json`: the target, its `spec_id`, the full protocol, the diagnostics and
the sha256 of the array), and only after the set passes its acceptance gates. Every run is
reproducible from its seed, and every set is stored interleaved so that its first `n` rows span
every chain.

| Targets | Generator | Sampler | Cache folder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dw4`, `lj13`, `lj55`, `ka-binary-N64-T0.2` | `gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target <name>` | non-reversible parallel tempering, HMC moves | `pt/` |
| the four peptides | `gen/md/generate.py --sequence <sequence>` | replica-exchange MD with solute tempering | `md/` |
| `ka-ternary-N44-T<T>` | `gen/swap_mc/generate.py --temperature <T>` | swap Monte Carlo | `swapmc/` |
| the posteriors but `brownian` | `gen/stan/generate.py --target <name>` | Stan's NUTS | `stan/` |
| `brownian` | `gen/exact/brownian.py` | exact: rejection on the scales, Gaussian locations | `exact/` |

## The samplers

The samplers behind the sets live in `gen/` with the generators: `gen/nrpt.py` (non-reversible
parallel tempering, its explorers and references, HMC), `gen/md/dynamics.py` (the solute split of
the peptides, and the peptide wrapper of the Langevin integrator) and `gen/diagnostics.py`
(integrated autocorrelation time, effective sample size, the spread of the chain means). The
kernels the train splits also use are in `samplingdb/targets/kernels.py` (Metropolis, MALA, the
batched BAOAB Langevin integrator), and `swap_monte_carlo` is in `samplingdb/targets/physics.py`.

`non_reversible_parallel_tempering(log_prob, score, x0, beta, explorer=None, n_rounds=10,
reference=None, ...)` is the scheme of Syed et al. 2022 (JRSS-B, Algorithms 1 to 4), the one
Pigeons.jl implements.

- **Swaps are deterministic**, alternating between even and odd pairs of adjacent levels, so a
  configuration walks the ladder ballistically. A swap permutes an index array; the configurations
  never move.
- **The schedule adapts.** The run proceeds in rounds of doubling length, and between rounds the
  levels are re-placed at equal increments of the measured communication barrier. Samples come from
  the final round, where nothing adapts.
- **Step sizes adapt** towards `target_acceptance` (0.65) with a damped update between rounds, after
  `calibrate_step_size` brackets each level's step size by geometric search before round 1.
- **A frozen run** passes a converged schedule and step sizes back in with `n_rounds=1`,
  `adaptation_gain=0.0` and any `n_scans`, with `n_burn` scans discarded first. That is how a
  calibrated ladder is reused across shards.

The local kernel comes from an explorer factory: `hmc_explorer`, or `dynamics_explorer`, which
wraps a molecular-dynamics integrator and carries velocities from one scan to the next. With
`reference=None` the path is `beta * log_prob`; otherwise the `beta = 0` end is a
`GaussianReference` (normalised, so the stepping stone gives `log Z`) or a `PotentialReference`, a
sub-potential of the target, which tempers only the rest of it: solute tempering (Wang, Friesner and
Berne 2011; Kamiya and Sugita 2018).
`return_diagnostics=True` returns the tuned schedule and step sizes, the global barrier `Lambda`,
the rejection rate per pair of levels, the acceptance per level and chain, the round trips per
chain, the stepping-stone `log_normaliser` and the final state.

`swap_monte_carlo(target, x, n_time_units, step_size=0.065, p_swap=0.0)` runs the move pool of
ParticlesMC (`TheDisorderedOrganization/ParticlesMC`) on a glass former. With probability
`1 - p_swap` a move displaces one particle by an isotropic Gaussian of scale `step_size`; otherwise
it exchanges two particles of the species pair a swap channel names: A<->C and B<->C for the
ternary mixture, never A<->B. One time unit is `n_particles` attempted moves. With `compile=True`
the move is compiled and every draw is made on the generator's device, which on a GPU runs about
nine times faster than the eager loop.

## What a set costs

A train split of 100 000 samples takes one GPU between about a second and a minute. The test split
of the same size, near-independent draws from the target, takes from seconds on the simplest
posteriors to more than 200 GPU-hours. The factor between the two follows how slowly the target
mixes: about 35 000 on the hexapeptide and the binary glass, 450 to 2 000 on the other peptides and
the direct LGCP, 60 to 130 on the particle clusters, Seeds and the supercooled ternary glass, and 5
to 35 on the logistic regressions, the whitened LGCP, Brownian and the ternary liquid. The
benchmark gives a sampler the cheap split to learn from and scores it against the expensive one.

The ten synthetic targets of Tier 0 are left out: their test splits are exact draws, their train
splits short runs of at most 200 moves, and each takes under a second on a GPU.

### How the costs are counted

- A train split is one uncached call of `build(name).to('cuda').generate_train_samples(100000)` on
  an RTX A6000, compilation included.
- A test split is the wall clock in the manifest of the published set, summed over its shards and
  the runs that extended them, plus the calibration run of the sets made by frozen shards. The
  peptide generator does not time the 50 ps of dynamics that equilibrate each shard; it is added at
  the shard's own rate.
- The rows of the three particle clusters are estimates: the published run's scans at the speed of
  the compiled sampler, measured on the A6000 with a short run at the published settings.
- The GPU sets ran on an RTX A6000 (the particle clusters, the ternary glass) or on H100 or A100
  GPUs (the peptides, the binary glass). A GPU-hour on any of them counts as one, which moves no
  ratio by more than a factor of three.
- The Stan sets and the exact Brownian sampler run on the CPU of one workstation, one core per Stan
  chain and 16 cores for the exact sampler, and are counted in core-hours. Their ratio divides the
  run's wall clock by the train split's. The exact sampler records no wall clock: its row is an
  estimate, its 86 batches of 200 000 proposals at the measured speed of one batch.
- A set larger or smaller than 100 000 rows is scaled linearly to 100 000.
- The ternary glass's chains and half of the binary glass's ladders start from equilibrated
  configurations of another run, whose cost is not counted.

of its GPU-hours, and the second about two thirds or less.

### Why the test splits cost so much

A test split has to be the target's equilibrium, sample by sample. Its generator has to cross the
target's barriers many times, run long enough to forget where it started, and thin its chains
until the kept states are close to independent, and every generator checks this before it writes
anything (see the sections below). The cost follows the slowest motion of the
target.

The particle clusters and the binary glass run non-reversible parallel tempering. On the clusters
the kept states are 2 to 16 scans apart, but each scan moves all 12 to 16 levels of a ladder by 2
to 32 HMC trajectories of 10 leapfrog steps, and the adaptive rounds that place the levels cost as
much as the collection round. The binary glass at T = 0.2 keeps one state every 458 752 scans of
ten levels, after 294 912 scans of burn-in: each of its 2 048 ladders runs about 7.4 million
scans, 200 force evaluations each, for the 15 states it contributes.

The peptides run replica-exchange molecular dynamics over six levels of solute tempering, at 1 fs
per step. Kept structures are 17 to 56 ps of dynamics apart at every level for the three short
peptides, and 270 ps apart for `AAAAAA`, whose slowest backbone torsions also need 1.6 ns of
burn-in. `AAAAAA`'s set is about 180 microseconds of dynamics in all.

The ternary glass is the model built for swap Monte Carlo (Jung et al. 2023), and the swaps keep
its test split affordable: at T = 0.32 each of 16 384 chains runs 240 000 time units against a
structural relaxation time of about 2 300. At T = 1.0 the mixture is a liquid that relaxes in 265
time units, and its test split costs about five times its train split.

Stan's NUTS writes nothing unless the largest R-hat is below 1.01, the bulk effective sample size
is above 90% of the draws, and no transition diverged. The logistic regressions are unimodal and
nearly Gaussian, and pass in seconds. `seeds` needs a smaller step size and keeps one draw in 32,
and the direct LGCP pays a dense 1600 x 1600 triangular solve at each of up to 1 023 leapfrog
steps per draw. The exact Brownian sampler accepts 0.6% of its proposals: 17 million proposals,
each a 20 x 20 Cholesky factorisation.

### Why the train splits are cheap

A train split has to cover the test split's modes and energies, and may get their proportions
wrong. Its chains need not cross barriers or reach equilibrium, and its states need not be
independent: 1 000 chains run in parallel, each for 2 000 MALA moves, 600 time units of
displacements or 17 ps of Langevin dynamics, keeping a state every 10 moves, 5 time units or 50
steps. The recipes place the chains in the modes directly. The mixtures draw a mode before they
move, the peptides rotate every residue into a backbone basin, the glasses start from basins made
by short Monte Carlo runs, and the particle chains run mostly hotter than the target, where they
cross its barriers quickly. A peptide's train split is 17 ns of dynamics in all, against 14
microseconds for the test split of `Ace-A-Nme` and 180 for that of `AAAAAA`.

## Sharded runs

The two tempered generators share these modes, and `gen/merge_shards.py` combines their output.
Both run the target's energy and score through `torch.compile`.

| Flag | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `--calibrate` | a short run that measures the barrier, the round-trip cost, the autocorrelation time and the throughput, and writes `plan/<target>_plan.json`; it skips the gates and writes no reference set |
| `--freeze` | reuses that plan's schedule and step sizes and adapts nothing |
| `--n_scans N`, `--n_burn M` | the length of the collection round and the burn-in in front of it |
| `--shard K --n_shards N` | writes `<name>_shardKK.npy` under seed `seed + K`, an independent run |
| `--hours H` | parallel tempering with `--freeze` only: repeats `--n_scans` segments, each continuing from the last one's level-ordered state, until H hours are spent, and rewrites the shard after every segment |
| `--start FILE --start_offset R` | parallel tempering only: starts every ladder from a configuration of FILE, rows R onwards, instead of the lattice and the melt; the manifest records the start group as `deep`, otherwise `melt` |
| `--resume` | parallel tempering with `--freeze` and `--shard` only: continues a shard from the ladder state it saved last (`<name>_shardKK_state.pt`), with no melt and no burn-in, appending to its stored states |

```sh
python gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target lj55 --calibrate
python gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target lj55 --freeze \
    --n_scans 4096 --n_burn 800 --thin 8 --shard 0 --n_shards 8        # once per shard
python gen/merge_shards.py --target lj55 --folder pt
```

Independent chains are independent, so shards merge exactly. `merge_shards.py` first checks every
shard's manifest: its `spec_id` must be the target's current one, and a shard of either tempered
generator must record that every pair of adjacent levels rejected fewer than 95% of its swaps and
that every chain made at least one round trip. It then concatenates the shards along the chain
axis and re-audits the whole set. It keeps every k-th stored state, for the smallest k at which
the merged set is worth more than 90% of its length by both the autocorrelation and the spread of
the chain means, and no observable's first tenth sits 5 or more standard errors from its last
half. When no k leaves two states per chain, it keeps the last state
of every chain. When the shards record two start groups, deep and melt, the per-chain means of
every audited observable must agree between the groups within 4 standard errors. It refuses to
write a set smaller than `--min_samples` (100 000), and it deletes the shard files afterwards
unless `--keep_shards` is given. The audited observables are the energy, the mean pair distance and
the 10%, 50% and 90% sorted pair-distance quantiles, plus the first four backbone torsions for the
peptides and the minority species' own pair distances for the glasses.

The drift check loses power as the states per chain fall: at three per chain it compares one state
against two. Read the means by stored index across every chain before trusting a sparse merge.

A frozen run needs a starting state near equilibrium, because a frozen step size on a high-energy
start is rejected forever. The generators start from their own lattices and minimised structures,
and `--n_burn` adds a margin on top.

## Parallel tempering: particles and the binary glass

`non_reversible_parallel_tempering` with an HMC explorer (10 leapfrog steps) and no reference: the
hottest level is the target at `beta_min`. Each target's ladder is pinned in `SETTINGS` and every
value can be overridden on the command line.

| Target | Levels | `beta_min` | HMC moves per scan | thin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dw4` | 12 | 0.1 | 2 | 16 |
| `lj13` | 16 | 0.1 | 32 | 2 |
| `lj55` | 12 | 0.5 | 8 | 8 |
| `ka-binary-N64-T0.2` | 10 | 1/3 | 2 | 16 384 |

LJ-55 starts at `beta_min = 0.5` because its target temperature `T* = 0.5` is already above the
cluster's melting point (0.294): hotter levels would spend most of the barrier on a dilute gas.

The binary glass at T = 0.2 is the slowest set and the smallest: 30 720 configurations, 15 from
each of 2 048 ladders. Its ladder is ten levels geometric in T from 0.6 to 0.2. The calibration
keeps that schedule and tunes only the step sizes: Λ = 4.60, swap rejections 0.49 to 0.52, one
round trip per ~860 scans. The slowest observables, the mean and median pair distance, need about
300 000 scans of burn-in and a spacing of about 300 000 scans for the merged set to be 90%
independent. Half the shards start from deep configurations of an earlier run (`--start`) and half
from the melt, so the merge's start-agreement gate tests the convergence directly. The published
set keeps one state every 458 752 scans (28 stored states of 16 384) after 294 912 scans of
burn-in: 91% independent, start drift 2.1 standard errors, and the deep and melt starts agree
within 1.6 standard errors on every audited observable.

```sh
python gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target ka-binary-N64-T0.2 --calibrate --thin 64 \
    --n_chains 128 --n_rounds 12
python gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target ka-binary-N64-T0.2 --freeze --n_chains 256 \
    --n_scans 65536 --n_burn 294912 --hours H --shard K \
    [--start deep.npy --start_offset 256K]                 # deep for half the shards
python gen/parallel_tempering/generate.py --target ka-binary-N64-T0.2 --freeze --n_chains 256 \
    --n_scans 65536 --hours H --shard K --resume            # to extend a shard
python gen/merge_shards.py --target ka-binary-N64-T0.2 --keep_shards
```

Before anything is written, every (level, chain) pair must accept more than 1% of its local moves,
every chain must make `--min_round_trips` (10) round trips, every pair of adjacent levels must
reject fewer than 95% of its swaps, and more than half of the stored states must be effectively
independent. A shard skips that last check and leaves it to the merge: one shard's estimate is
too noisy to gate on, and a shard that failed it would lose its states. Independence is measured
on the energy, the mean pair distance and the 10%, 50% and 90% quantiles of the sorted pair
distances.

## Replica-exchange MD: peptides

`non_reversible_parallel_tempering` with `dynamics_explorer` around `run_batched_dynamics` of
`gen/md/dynamics.py`: BAOAB at 1 fs with the real masses and the system's own friction.

- **Solute tempering.** The reference is `PotentialReference(harmonic_log_prob, harmonic_score)`, so
  `beta` scales the torsions, the nonbonded terms and the Born solvation, and the bonds and angles
  stay at full strength. They carry most of the energy's spread and none of the conformational
  barriers. `--tempering global` scales the whole potential instead.
- **The potential is tempered, not the thermostat.** Each level integrates `kT beta score` at the
  target's own `kT`, so every level is Maxwell at the same temperature, velocities are carried from
  scan to scan, and a swap needs no velocity rescaling. The move is unadjusted, so the timestep is
  fixed rather than adapted, and it is a bias: at 1 fs Ace-A-Nme's mean energy sits 0.10 ± 0.03 kT
  above the zero-timestep limit, which an exact sampler reads as an `energy_w1` of about 0.1 kT
  against the set.
- **Starts.** Every replica starts from the minimised released structure and is equilibrated at its
  own level for `--equilibrate_ps` (50 ps) before the first round.

`SETTINGS` in `gen/md/generate.py` holds the calibration defaults: 6 levels from `beta_min = 0.4`,
512 replicas (170 for `AAAAAA`), thin 96 and 0.1 ps of MD per scan. Each published set was made by
frozen shards of that calibration (`--freeze`), with these values:

| Sequence | Levels | `beta_min` | Replicas per shard | Shards | MD per scan | Burn-in | thin | Stored per replica |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Ace-A-Nme` | 6 | 0.4 | 1 000 | 10 | 0.1 ps | 172 scans | 172 scans | 10 |
| `AAA` | 6 | 0.4 | 1 000 | 10 | 0.1 ps | 211 scans | 211 scans | 10 |
| `Ace-AAA-Nme` | 6 | 0.4 | 1 000 | 10 | 0.1 ps | 561 scans | 561 scans | 10 |
| `AAAAAA` | 6 | 0.4 | 113 | 15 | 0.1 ps | 16 000 scans | 2 700 scans | 59 |

The merge kept every stored state: 100 000 structures for each of the first three and 100 005 for
`AAAAAA`, between 92% and 98% of them effectively independent.

Before anything is written, every pair of adjacent levels must reject fewer than 95% of its swaps,
every replica must complete a round trip, more than half of the stored states must be effectively
independent (on the pair distances, their quantiles and the first four torsions), and the energy
must not move by 5 or more standard errors between the two halves of the collection round.

## Swap Monte Carlo: the ternary glass

16 384 independent chains on one GPU, compiled, with `p_swap = 0.2` and displacements of scale
0.065. Each chain starts from a configuration of an earlier equilibrium set of the same target
(`--start`, every `--start_stride`-th row), is burned in, and then stores a state every `spacing`
time units. The spacing is finer than the decorrelation time on purpose: the merge measures how
many stored states to skip.

| Temperature | Burn-in | Spacing | States per chain | Wall clock on one A6000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 | 4 000 | 400 | 20 | 3 minutes |
| 0.32 | 20 000 | 5 000 | 44 | 1 hour |

```sh
python gen/swap_mc/generate.py --temperature 1.0 --start <earlier set>.npy --start_stride 6
python gen/merge_shards.py --target ka-ternary-N44-T1.0 --folder swapmc --min_samples 100000
```

The structural relaxation time read off the trajectories, following each particle through the
swaps, is about 2 300 time units at T = 0.32 and 265 at T = 1.0. The merge gates the set as for
parallel tempering, on the energy and the pair distances, and the shard's manifest records the
specific heat `c_V = Var(E) / (N T^2)` of each half of the stored states: c_V converges only on
independent configurations (Jung et al. 2024), so the two halves must agree.

## Stan posteriors

Three `.stan` files cover the six posteriors Stan samples, and `generate.py` builds each model's
data block from the torch target, so the two cannot drift apart. Needs `cmdstanpy` and CmdStan.

```sh
python gen/stan/generate.py --target ionosphere
```

The models declare their parameters in this library's unconstrained coordinates and add every
Jacobian by hand, so Stan samples exactly the density `log_prob` evaluates and its draws need no
transformation. Before sampling, `generate.py` compares Stan's log density with the torch one,
constant included, at eight random points, and stops if they differ by more than 1e-9.

| Targets | `adapt_delta` | `max_treedepth` | Chains | thin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| logistic regressions | 0.8 | 10 | 4 | 1 |
| `seeds` | 0.95 | 12 | 4 | 32 |
| `lgcp`, `lgcp-whitened` | 0.8 | 10 | 16 | 3 |

Nothing is written unless the largest R-hat is below 1.01, the smallest bulk ESS is above 90% of
the kept draws, and there are no divergent transitions. The draws are shuffled with a seeded
permutation, so the first `n` rows span every chain. The direct LGCP parameterisation is the slow
one: its run takes 29 hours on one machine, against 30 minutes for the whitened one.

## Exact sampler: Brownian motion

Given its two scales, the Brownian motion posterior is Gaussian in the 30 locations, so it
factorises exactly. `gen/exact/brownian.py` proposes both log scales from their N(0, 2^2) priors
and accepts each pair with probability L / max L, where L is the marginal likelihood with the
locations integrated out and its maximum is found on a grid and refined. It then draws the
locations from their Gaussian conditional, in precision form. Before sampling it checks that the
factorised density equals `log_prob` up to one constant, to 1e-8 across 1000 random points, and
every proposal is checked against the bound.

```sh
python gen/exact/brownian.py
```

## Publishing

The sets are published to the Hugging Face dataset `h2o64/samplingdb`, laid out like the cache:
`pt/`, `md/`, `stan/`, `exact/` and `swapmc/`, each array next to its manifest, with a dataset card.
The same release holds the default train split of every target under `train/`, written on a GPU by
`gen/train/generate.py` and named `<name>_<spec_id>_<recipe>.npy`, so a changed recipe is a new
file.
The library pins two things in `samplingdb/data.py`: `REFERENCE_REVISION`, the tag it reads, and
`REFERENCE_CHECKSUMS`, the sha256 of every file published at that tag. A tag is never moved, so
every version of the library keeps reading the bytes it was tested against.

`gen/publish/publish.py` does the whole release. Run without arguments, it uploads nothing:

1. It builds every catalogued target and matches each registered file to the target that reads it.
   A registered file that no target reads any more means a density changed under it, and stops the
   release. It lists the cached sets that are not registered, with the line that would register
   each, and the targets that have no set.
2. It checks every registered set in the cache: the sha256 against the registry and the manifest,
   the manifest's target and `spec_id`, the dimension, and that every value is finite.
3. It writes the dataset card, from `gen/publish/card.md` and the manifests, to
   `$SAMPLINGDB_CACHE/publish/<tag>/`.

With `--upload` it then logs in to Hugging Face (asking for a write token the first time), creates
the repo, commits each set with its manifest, commits the card and tags the release. An upload that
stops partway resumes when rerun, since each set is its own commit and what is already there is
skipped. A registered set whose bytes differ from the file on the main branch replaces it, with its
manifest; the earlier tags keep serving the old file. When the tag exists already, it is checked
and left where it is, and only what the main branch lacks, such as a newer card, is added. Last, it
checks the tag against the registry and downloads the smallest set through the library's own code
into an empty cache. `--check_release` runs only that last check.

```sh
SAMPLINGDB_CACHE=<cache holding the sets> python gen/publish/publish.py            # check
SAMPLINGDB_CACHE=<cache holding the sets> python gen/publish/publish.py --upload   # publish
```

The repo has to belong to the account that runs the upload. If it is not `h2o64`, change
`REFERENCE_REPO` in `samplingdb/data.py` before the first release.

Adding a set:

1. Generate and merge it as above. It lands in the cache as `<folder>/<name>_<spec_id>.npy`, and
   `test_samples()` reads it from there at once.
2. Audit it, including the means by stored index for a sparse merge.
3. Run `publish.py`, which prints the line to add to `REFERENCE_CHECKSUMS`. Add it, set
   `REFERENCE_REVISION` to the next tag, and add a row to the card's table of tags.
4. Run `publish.py --upload`, then commit and push the code. Until the push, users keep reading
   the previous tag, which still serves every file it served before.

A set whose density changed has a new `spec_id`, so it is a new file under a new name: register the
new file and remove the old line from `REFERENCE_CHECKSUMS`. A set regenerated for an unchanged
density keeps its name: update its line and bump the tag. A user's cache still holds the old bytes
under that name, so `test_samples()` checks a cached registered set against its sha256 once and
raises, naming the fix, when it comes from another tag.

## Audits

| Script | Question |
| --- | --- |
| `gen/audit/barrier.py --target <name>` | where on the path the communication barrier sits, and what each candidate `beta_min` would cost |
| `gen/audit/decorrelation.py --target <name> --folder <folder>` | how many independent samples a generated set holds |
| `gen/audit/stationarity.py --sequence <sequence>` | whether a released split is an equilibrium sample of this library's density |

Choosing a ladder for a new target:

- Run `barrier.py` first. One round trip costs about `n_levels (2 + 2 Lambda)` scans, so when round
  trips are what decorrelates a configuration its cost is about `8 Lambda^3` local moves at the
  optimal `2 Lambda` levels, and the hot end of the path is the expensive choice. When the local
  kernel decorrelates faster than a round trip, as on LJ-55, the cost is linear in `Lambda`.
- Set `thin` to the autocorrelation time measured in scans, and iterate with `decorrelation.py`:
  thinning a fat-tailed autocorrelation does not divide it by the thinning factor. Thinning up to
  that time costs no effective samples.
- Measure the autocorrelation time only on observables the metrics can see: energies, pair distances
  and their sorted quantiles, torsions. Translation, rotation and particle permutation diffuse freely,
  so raw coordinates and labelled pairs read much longer times than the set has. The estimate needs a
  collection round of at least about ten round trips.

## OpenMM check

`gen/openmm_check/compare.py` checks `TorchForceField` against OpenMM's `Reference` platform force
group by force group, in float64 on frames of the reference set, and stops unless the largest
energy error is within 1e-6 of the rms energy and the largest force error within 1e-4 of the rms
force component; on 256 frames of each peptide the worst are 9e-8 and 2e-8. It then checks that the
score has a finite second derivative, and times the batched float32 torch path against every OpenMM
platform. `openmm_bridge.py` is the one-configuration-at-a-time OpenMM evaluator it compares
against. The CUDA platform installs with `pip install "openmm[cuda13]"`.
